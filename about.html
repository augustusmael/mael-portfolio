My Philosophy

My approach is to ask better questions before I reach for answers.

I ask better questions because better questions are what get you to the best possible answer — not just an answer. Growth depends on accepting an uncomfortable fact: the answer you're working with right now might be poor, workable but not the best way to do the work, or live the life, in front of you. Standing still with "good enough" is how you stop growing.

The Socratic method is how I put that into practice. It means questioning the assumptions that were already baked in before a conversation even started, so the information that comes out is as unbiased and true as it can be — regardless of who it came from. I don't expect to land on 100% truth. I do expect to be able to say, honestly, that a conclusion holds up beyond reasonable doubt, because it survived extreme scrutiny rather than being taken on faith.

That's exactly where this connects to operations, quality, and automation. All three run on the same underlying discipline: logic. A workflow is an argument in disguise — a set of assumptions, steps, and algorithms claiming to produce the best outcome. Questioning it the way I'd question any claim is how you actually find out whether that's true, and asking better questions is how you build the pattern that replaces it.

So the process for me is always the same, whether it's a philosophical claim or a broken process: ask, scrutinize, arrive at the best answer available, then build it into the system. Broadcasting and travelling keep me asking outside of work too — but the destination is always operations, quality, and automation, built by someone who treats "why" as the first question, not the last.

My Story

I'm a Computer Engineering student at the Technological Institute of the Philippines (TIP QC), and the same instinct that shows up in my coursework and side projects — look at a system, find where it's fragile, make it reliable — is what carried over the moment I started working professionally.

That started on the floor. I handled customer concerns and calls at Alorica Philippines in mid-2023, then moved to TaskUs Philippines in April 2024 as a Customer Service Representative, handling high-volume chat interactions. Both roles were graded daily on quality, and I treated every score the way I'd treat any claim worth questioning — not just meeting the KPI, but understanding exactly why a response scored the way it did.

That habit is what got me regularized and then promoted almost immediately after: about six months in, on my supervisors' recommendation, I moved into the Subject Matter Expert role in November 2024 — leading calibration sessions with Quality Analysts and Team Leaders, coaching close to 20 agents in real time, and owning the reporting, documentation, and root-cause analysis that keep a campaign's quality consistent rather than lucky.

Broadcasting and event production, on the side, kept that same discipline sharp in a different setting — live, no room for a second take. Different stage, same question I keep asking everywhere: is this actually the best way to do it, and how do I know? That question is what built the case for operations, quality, and automation as the work I actually want to do.
